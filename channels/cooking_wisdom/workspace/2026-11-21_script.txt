=== THEME ===
The sa-shi-su-se-so order explained: why Japanese seasoning sequence (sugar, salt, vinegar, soy sauce, miso) changes the final flavor.

=== TITLE ===
Why Japanese Chefs Always Season in This Secret Order

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Learn the traditional Japanese seasoning sequence: Sa-Shi-Su-Se-So! Sugar penetrates ingredients slowly and needs a head start, while salt follows so it doesn't block sweetness. Heat-sensitive soy sauce and miso are saved for last to protect their delicate aromas. Master timing to transform your stewed dishes!

=== SUBTITLES & NARRATION SCRIPTS ===
[Slide 1]
  Subtitle: Why season in a secret five-letter order?
  Narration: Sugar penetrates slowly, so it enters the pot first. Salt comes next.

[Slide 2]
  Subtitle: Salt blocks sugar if added too early.
  Narration: Early salt draws out moisture, which blocks sugar from seeping inside ingredients.

[Slide 3]
  Subtitle: Protect delicate aromas from long heat.
  Narration: Vinegar softens with heat, so add it later to guard its fresh fragrance.

[Slide 4]
  Subtitle: Soy sauce and miso belong last.
  Narration: Soy sauce and miso aromas die fast in heat, so keep them for last.

[Slide 5]
  Subtitle: Flavor enters deeply as food cools down.
  Narration: Simmered dishes absorb most flavor while cooling, so judge seasoning after resting.

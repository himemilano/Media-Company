=== THEME ===
The sa-shi-su-se-so order explained: why Japanese seasoning sequence (sugar, salt, vinegar, soy sauce, miso) changes the final flavor.

=== TITLE ===
Why Japanese Chefs Season in Sa-Shi-Su-Se-So Order

=== DESCRIPTION ===
Master the traditional Japanese seasoning sequence known as Sa-Shi-Su-Se-So! Sugar goes in first because it penetrates slowly, followed by salt, vinegar, soy sauce, and delicate miso at the very end. Learn how timing and resting food affects flavor absorption. #JapaneseCooking #ChefTips #SaShiSuSeSo #CulinarySecrets #Washoku

=== SUBTITLES & NARRATION SCRIPTS ===
[Slide 1]
  Subtitle: Why chefs use the secret seasoning order?
  Narration: Sugar goes first because it penetrates slowly. Salt follows so sugar isn't blocked.

[Slide 2]
  Subtitle: Vinegar changes with time and heat.
  Narration: Add vinegar late to protect aroma, or early to soften tough bones.

[Slide 3]
  Subtitle: Save soy sauce and miso last.
  Narration: Soy sauce and delicate miso lose their fragrant aromas under prolonged heat.

[Slide 4]
  Subtitle: Food absorbs sauce while cooling down.
  Narration: Hot simmered food pulls in flavor quickly while cooling down, not boiling.

[Slide 5]
  Subtitle: Timing seasonings changes the entire dish.
  Narration: Hold back fresh aromatics until the end to keep every flavor alive.
